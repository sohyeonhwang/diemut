# Code of Conduct

diemut is a small project, and we want it to be a welcoming place for everyone who uses it, reports a problem, or contributes to it, whatever their background, experience level or identity.

## Our standards

We expect everyone taking part in the project to:

- Be kind and patient, especially with people who are new to Python, Git or open source. Many diemut users aren't programmers, and "how do I install this?" is a perfectly good question.
- Assume good intent, and ask before assuming someone meant harm.
- Give and accept feedback on code and ideas graciously. Critique the work, not the person.
- Respect people's time and privacy. Don't share anyone's personal information, or audio and transcripts someone posts while asking for help, without their permission.
- Own your mistakes, apologize to anyone affected, and learn from them.

The following is not acceptable:

- Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments, including about someone's gender, sexual orientation, disability, appearance, race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, age or level of experience.
- Sexualized language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Threats, or encouraging anyone else to harm someone.
- Trolling, deliberately derailing discussions, or repeatedly pushing a topic after being asked to stop.
- Any other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces: issues, pull requests, discussions, code review, and any other channel run for diemut. It also applies when you represent the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, please contact me at **sohyeon@princeton.edu**. All reports will be reviewed promptly and kept as confidential as possible. I won't retaliate against anyone who makes a report in good faith.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for upholding this code. Depending on what happened, they may:

1. **Talk to the person privately** to explain what was wrong, and ask them to apologize or change their behavior.
2. **Give a warning** with clear expectations for the future.
3. **Temporarily ban the person** from participating in project spaces.
4. **Permanently ban the person**, for serious or repeated violations.

Maintainers may also remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code and issues that don't follow this code.
